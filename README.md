# Retina Woods
https://aidanlambournecreatesstuff-ops.github.io/ma-1800-web-art-project-code/

# Project Description
Retina Woods is a web-based abstract artwork that presents the social transparency and access of the internet under a horror lens. The non-linear connection of HTML pages creates an environment of being lost. The scary images I edited and inserted into the project add to the horror aesthetic. The drone loop was intended to be seamless, however I found the interupted drone to prove much more anxiety-provoking. 
The titles of the forest pages can be decoded from hexadecimal to reflect a small poem that the users can put together to make the line "They will always see you, no matter how far your legs run." 
The bonus, secret pages have titles that reflect their own messages.
I scrambled the urls of the pages to hexidecimal code. This will mean the user can't navigate the page through the url, meaning they are truly lost and at mercy of the forest. The urls of the forest pages can be decoded and arranged to make the line, 'Why run? Accept your place here forever and ever for all time.' 
The urls of the bonus, secret pages can be decoded and arranged to make messages unique to their own messages. 
- 'Please Stay' (Page 13)
- 'it never ends' (Page 14)
- 'pressure' (Page 15)
- 'welcome back' (Page 16)
- 'bubbling calm' (Page17)

# Case Studies
I was immediately inspired by JODI’s wwwwwwwww.jodi.org (1995) for the creative and abstract use of hyperlinks. Where her artwork makes you feel confused as you delve deeper into the pages, I wanted the users to feel uncomforable and afraid. 

I was also inspired by Vivian Selbo's Vertical Blanking Interval (1996) for the interesting use of imagery. The first page tells a story without words, where text only appears on subsequent pages through exploring. I wanted to create a narrative that isn't led by words.

To create the talking audio files, I input text I wanted into the <a href="https://tetyys.com/SAPI4/"> Onilne Microsoft TTS Generator</a>. I used the Brutus voice and modulated the speed and pitch as necessary. 

To create the audio drones, I used <a href="https://bandlab.com"> Bandlab</a>, using the SawSine instrument in a series of low pitches.
